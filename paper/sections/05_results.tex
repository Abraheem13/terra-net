\section{Performance evaluation}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports the experimental setting and the results of the
evaluation described in Section~\ref{sec:methods}. The results are
presented in the order of the pipeline of Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}: the
corpora and the precision of the tile labels, the transfer of tile
parameters, the network-level evaluation and its decomposition, the
mixed-path compositions, survey design and certified coverage, and the
robustness analyses. Every table and figure is generated from the released
result files, and every comparison is accompanied by the city-level
statistics of Section~\ref{sec:robust}, which are collected in
Section~\ref{sec:res-stats}.

\subsection{Experimental setting}
\label{sec:setting}

The setting is fixed by the configuration files of the released pipeline,
so that a reader can regenerate every result under the same conditions.
All corpora were generated and all analyses were executed on a virtual
machine with four cores of an Intel Xeon processor at
\SI{2.1}{\giga\hertz} and \SI{15}{\giga\byte} of memory under Ubuntu~24.04,
without a GPU. Ray tracing uses Sionna~RT~\sionnaVersion{} on the LLVM
backend of Mitsuba~3.9; the analysis uses Python~3.13 with
scikit-learn~1.9, LightGBM~4.7 and PyTorch~2.14. Table~\ref{tab:settings}
lists the parameters of the corpus generation, the models and the
evaluation. The ray tracing of the main corpus took \rtHours{}\,h on the
\rtBackend{} backend (\rtSecPerSite{}\,s per site); the analysis that
follows it, from the tile labels to the certificates, took
\rtTotalHours{}\,h in total on \rtCpus{} cores (Table~\ref{tab:runtime}),
of which the \rtHeaviestStage{} account for \rtHeaviestHours{}\,h.

The evaluation metrics are those of Section~\ref{sec:neteval}, and are
summarised as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Parameter error:} root-mean-square error (RMSE) of the
transferred exponent $\gamma$ and intercept $P_{L_0}$ over the tiles of the
held-out city.
\item \textbf{Path-loss error:} RMSE and bias over the ray-traced links, and
the censored RMSE of Eq.~\eqref{eq:plmetrics} over all (pixel, site) pairs.
\item \textbf{Link existence:} precision and recall of the predicted links
below the cut-off, and the share of pixels whose reached state is predicted
correctly.
\item \textbf{Serving site:} share of the reached pixels assigned to the
correct serving site, Eq.~\eqref{eq:assoc}.
\item \textbf{SINR and coverage:} mean absolute SINR error,
Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance between the SINR distributions, coverage
agreement and coverage-share error at \SI{130}{\decibel}, and the error of
the mean spectral efficiency, Eq.~\eqref{eq:sinrmetrics}.
\item \textbf{Cell edges:} displacement of the predicted cell edges and
relative error of their total length, Eq.~\eqref{eq:edges}.
\item \textbf{Planning regret and dimensioning:} coverage lost by selecting
$K$ sites on the predicted map instead of the true map,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:regret}, and the number of sites the predicted map claims to
need for \covTarget\% coverage together with their true coverage.
\item \textbf{Certified coverage:} share of the pixels claimed covered under
the certificate of Proposition~\ref{prop:certify} and share claimed but not
covered.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Corpora, solver convergence and tile labels}
\label{sec:res-corpus}
\label{sec:res-labels}

Before any model is compared, two properties of the data fix what a model
can be expected to achieve: the Monte-Carlo noise of the ray-traced truth and
the precision of the tile labels fitted from it. Both are quantified here,
together with the descriptive statistics of the corpora.

The corpus contains \nCities{} cities, \nBuildings{} buildings,
\nSites{} sites and \nSamples{} ray-traced links (Table~\ref{tab:corpus});
\nTiles{} tiles have at least \minLinks{} links and are labelled.
Figure~\ref{fig:city} shows one city. The share of buildings whose height
is imputed from the city median ranges from \impMin\% (\impMinCity) to
\impMax\% (\impMaxCity). The radio-map solver is a Monte-Carlo method: a
cell that no ray reaches reports zero power, so the set of reached cells
and their values depend on the ray budget. Figure~\ref{fig:convergence}
compares budgets with a reference run of \convRef{} rays for \convNSites{}
sites in \convNCities{} cities. At the \rtSamples{} rays per site used for
the corpus, path loss below \SI{120}{\decibel} agrees with the reference to
at most \SI{\convRmsOneTwentyMax}{\decibel} RMS and path loss below
\SI{140}{\decibel} to \convRmsOneFortyMin--\SI{\convRmsOneFortyMax}{\decibel},
with a mean difference of at most \SI{\convBiasOneFortyMax}{\decibel}; the
number of cells reached below \SI{140}{\decibel} is at least
\convCellsOneFortyMin\% of the reference, and below the cut-off at least
\convCellsOneFiftyMin\%. These values are the noise floor of the truth:
no model can be expected to match the corpus more closely than
\SIrange{\convRmsOneFortyMin}{\convRmsOneFortyMax}{\decibel} near the
cut-off, and up to \convRmsOneFiftyMax{}\,dB for the faintest links.

Table~\ref{tab:fits} summarises the labels. City medians of the tile
exponent range from \gammaMedMin{} (\gammaMedMinCity) to \gammaMedMax{}
(\gammaMedMaxCity); their standard deviation across cities, \betweenSd, is
\betweenWithinRatio{} times the typical standard deviation of the tiles
within a city (\withinSdMin--\withinSdMax), so most of the variation of the
tile exponent lies within cities rather than between them
(Fig.~\ref{fig:corpus}a). The fit residual of the log-distance law inside a
tile is \fitRmseMin--\SI{\fitRmseMax}{\decibel}. The city-wide exponent of
a single law over all links is below the free-space value in
\nCityGammaBelowTwo{} cities (\cityGammaMinCity: \cityGammaMin), the joint
effect of high sites and of the censoring of far links
(Section~\ref{sec:res-robust}); the tile labels, fitted inside
\SI{100}{\metre}, do not show this artefact. Without shrinkage, the OLS
exponents span \olsMin{} to \olsMax{}, the ill-conditioning of
Section~\ref{sec:labels}; the empirical-Bayes estimator of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eb} (median weight \shrinkMedian) narrows this to \ebMin{} to
\ebMax. The descriptors carry signal but no single one explains much of the
variation (Fig.~\ref{fig:corpus}b): the strongest correlation with the
exponent is $r=\topDescR$, for \topDesc.

Label precision is the decisive quantity for everything that follows
(Fig.~\ref{fig:noise}). The standard error of the exponent under independent
residuals is \seIid{} (RMS over tiles); the cluster-robust error of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cr} with sites as clusters is \seCluster, \seRatio{} times
larger, and the split-half estimate of Eq.~\eqref{eq:split} extrapolated to
the full data is \seSplit. For the intercept the corresponding errors are
\plSeIid{} and \SI{\plSeCluster}{\decibel}. The exponent of a tile
therefore depends strongly on which sites serve it, and a label error of
this size is comparable to the differences between cities
(Table~\ref{tab:fits}).

\subsection{Transfer of tile parameters}
\label{sec:res-loco}
\label{sec:res-kernel}
\label{sec:res-fewshot}

This subsection answers the first part of the transfer question: how well
the tile parameters of a city that took no part in training can be
predicted from its descriptors, zero-shot and with a small survey. The
operators of Section~\ref{sec:operators} are compared under the nested
protocol of Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}, the kernel operator is placed relative
to Proposition~\ref{prop:regimes}, and the few-shot corrections of
Section~\ref{sec:fewshot} are evaluated against the number of surveyed
tiles.

Table~\ref{tab:loco} and Fig.~\ref{fig:loco} report the exponent error on
every held-out city. The source median attains \rmseMedian; kernel
transfer \rmseKernel; the Gaussian process with learned length scales
\rmseGpArd; the learned regressors \rmseRidge{} (ridge), \rmseMlp{} (MLP),
\rmseEncoder{} (neural encoder) and \rmseGbdt{} (GBDT). The
domain-adaptation variants do not improve on their base learners (CORAL
\rmseCoral{} against ridge \rmseRidge; GBDT-IW \rmseGbdtIw{} against GBDT
\rmseGbdt), and the standard models are worse than the median (3GPP UMa
\rmseUmaLos{} for line of sight and \rmseUmaNlos{} for non-line of sight,
COST-231 Hata \rmseCost). The lowest mean error is that of the
\bestZeroShot, but the learned operators differ by less than their spread
across cities. For the intercept at \SI{\refDist}{\metre} the learned
operators reduce the error from \SI{\plRmseMedian}{\decibel} (median) to
\SI{\plRmseGbdt}{\decibel} (GBDT). The hyper-parameters selected by the
inner loop are listed in Table~\ref{tab:hparams}. At the level of cities
(Table~\ref{tab:stats}), GBDT changes the error against the median by
\dGbdtMedian{} (95\% CI [\loGbdtMedian, \hiGbdtMedian], $p=\pGbdtMedian$,
better in \nGbdtMedian{} of \nFolds{} cities) and against kernel transfer by
\dGbdtKernel{} ([\loGbdtKernel, \hiGbdtKernel]); kernel transfer against
the median gives \dKernelMedian{} ([\loKernelMedian, \hiKernelMedian],
$p=\pKernelMedian$), whereas learning the metric of the same kernel gives
\dGpKernel{} ([\loGpKernel, \hiGpKernel], $p=\pGpKernel$, better in
\nGpKernel{} of \nFolds{} cities), \holmGpKernel{} after Holm's correction.
Over the \nZeroShotTotal{} comparisons with the source median,
\zeroShotSigBetterWord{} of the improvements \zeroShotSigBetterVerb{} Holm's correction at
eleven cities\zeroShotSigBetterParen, although the learned operators are
better than the median in \learnedBetterMin{} to \learnedBetterMax{} of
\nFolds{} cities; the significant differences in the other direction are
\zeroShotSigWorseList. The hardest target is \hardCity, where the best
operator (\hardCityBestOp) reaches \hardCityBest.

Table~\ref{tab:degeneracy} places the tuned kernel operator relative to
Proposition~\ref{prop:regimes}. The selected bandwidths are
$\sigma=\sigmaMin$--$\sigmaMax$ and the median $\epsilon_u$ ranges from
\epsMedianMin{} to \epsMedianMax: \fracEpsRegime\% of the target tiles
satisfy the condition of part~(a), and the largest single weight has median
\wmaxMedian, so the operator is not a nearest-neighbour copy either. Where
the largest bandwidth is selected (\nSigmaLargest{} of \nFolds{} targets),
it averages over up to \neffFracMax\% of the source tiles and its
predictions spread over as little as \spreadMin{} of the spread of the
target labels: it approaches the source mean although part~(a) does not
hold. Across the targets the prediction spread is
\spreadMin--\spreadMax{} of that of the labels. The disadvantage of the
operator is the noise-transmission term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp} combined
with an isotropic distance in which every descriptor counts equally; the
Gaussian process of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ard}, which learns one length scale per
descriptor, is the kernel operator that removes most of the gap to the
learned regressors.

Surveying target tiles helps the tile exponent little at small $k$
(Table~\ref{tab:kshot}, Fig.~\ref{fig:kshot}). The plain mean-residual
correction is worse than the zero-shot model at $k=\fsPlainWorseKs$ (GBDT at
$k=3$: \fsGbdtBiasThree{} against \fsZeroGbdt{} zero-shot): the labels of
the anchors carry the site-dependent error measured above, and a correction
fitted to a few of them adds more variance than it removes. The
empirical-Bayes correction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fewshot}, which weights the
anchors by their precision and shrinks the correction when the inner cities
show no systematic bias, is worse than the zero-shot model \fsEbNeverWorse{}
(GBDT at $k=3$: \fsGbdtEbThree; at $k=10$: \fsTenGbdt, \dFewEbGbdt{} against
the plain correction, [\loFewEbGbdt, \hiFewEbGbdt]), but its gain is small:
with ten random tiles it improves the GBDT error in \fsImprovedTen{} of
\nFolds{} cities, by \fsGainGbdt\% on average (\dFewGbdt, [\loFewGbdt,
\hiFewGbdt]), and with $k=\fsKmax$ tiles by \fsGainMaxGbdt\% (\fsMaxGbdt).
The anchors measure the site-dependent label, not an environmental constant,
so even many of them move the transferred exponent little. The
precision-weighted residual model of Eq.~\eqref{eq:resid} gives
\fsGbdtResidTen{} at $k=10$. Contiguous survey areas are worse than random
tiles (GBDT at $k=10$: \fsGbdtPatchTen{}), because neighbouring tiles repeat
the same information, and the facility-location design of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:facility}, which spreads the survey over the descriptor space
without any measurement, gives \fsGbdtDesignTen{} at $k=10$
(\dFewDesignGbdt{} against random tiles, [\loFewDesignGbdt,
\hiFewDesignGbdt], better in \nFewDesignGbdt{} of \nFolds{} cities).

\subsection{Network-level evaluation}
\label{sec:res-network}

The parameter errors of the previous subsection matter only through the
quantities a planner derives from the map. This subsection scores every
predictor over the complete receiver grid of the labelled tiles, through
path loss, link existence, serving site, SINR, coverage, cell edges,
dimensioning and the regret of a site plan (Section~\ref{sec:neteval}).

Table~\ref{tab:network} and Fig.~\ref{fig:network} give the means over the
held-out cities and Fig.~\ref{fig:cityres} the city-level values of the
three reference predictors. The first row of Table~\ref{tab:network} is the
tile model with the true labels (oracle), which isolates the error of the
tile model itself. The oracle has a path-loss error of
\SI{\netOracle}{\decibel} over the ray-traced links and
\SI{\censOracle}{\decibel} over all pairs clipped at the cut-off, selects
the correct serving site for \assocOracle\% of the pixels (the nearest-site
rule of Section~\ref{sec:transfer}) and misjudges the SINR by
\SI{\sinrOracle}{\decibel} on average. Transferred tile parameters reduce
the path-loss error of the source median from \SI{\netMedian}{\decibel} to
\SI{\netGbdt}{\decibel} (GBDT; \dNetGbdtMedian{} dB, better in
\nNetGbdtMedian{} of \nFolds{} cities) but remain above the oracle, and a
plan of $K=5$ sites made on their map loses \regretGbdt\% of the pixels
against \regretOracle\% for the oracle.

Site-aware link transfer removes the ceiling of the tile model. Its
path-loss error is \SI{\netLink}{\decibel} (\linkMin{} to
\SI{\linkMax}{\decibel} across cities, Fig.~\ref{fig:cityres}a),
\SI{\gainLinkGbdt}{\decibel} below tile transfer with GBDT (95\% CI
[\loNetLinkGbdt, \hiNetLinkGbdt], better in \nNetLinkGbdt{} of \nFolds{}
cities) and \SI{\gainLinkOracle}{\decibel} below the tile model with the
\emph{true} labels ([\loNetLinkOracle, \hiNetLinkOracle], \nNetLinkOracle{}
of \nFolds). It selects the correct serving site for \assocLink\% of the
pixels (\dNetLinkAssoc{} percentage points against GBDT, in
\nNetLinkAssoc{} of \nFolds{} cities), misjudges the SINR by
\SI{\sinrLink}{\decibel} on average and loses \regretLink\% of the pixels
when planning $K=5$ sites, against \regretGbdt\% for GBDT tile transfer and
\regretOracle\% for the true tile labels (Fig.~\ref{fig:cityres}c). Its
hurdle decides the reach of every site: it predicts the reached set of
pixels correctly for \reachLink\% of them, with a link precision of
\linkPrecLink\% and a recall of \linkRecLink\% (Table~\ref{tab:hurdle});
the regressor alone, which keeps every link, has the same path-loss error
over the ray-traced links but a censored error of
\SI{\censLinkReg}{\decibel} and a planning regret of \regretLinkReg\%,
because it lets distant, obstructed sites cover pixels they do not reach.
The model relies on the propagation features, which account for
\linkFeatShare\% of the gain of its trees (Table~\ref{tab:importance}); the
single most important feature is the \topLinkFeat{} (\topLinkFeatShare\%),
and the three most important together account for \topThreeLinkShare\%.

Table~\ref{tab:mobility} turns the maps into mobility and dimensioning
quantities. Every tile model draws the nearest-site cell pattern of
Section~\ref{sec:transfer}: its cell edges lie \SI{\edgeGbdt}{\metre} from
the true edges on average (\SI{\edgeOracle}{\metre} with the true labels),
and their total length is in error by \edgeLenGbdt\%, because the
ray-traced serving pattern is fragmented by buildings and a route crosses
many more cell edges than the nearest-site pattern suggests. Link transfer
places the edges \SI{\edgeLink}{\metre} from the truth with an edge-length
error of \edgeLenLink\%. For dimensioning, the truth needs on average
\sitesNeedTrue{} of the \nSitesPerCity{} sites to cover \covTarget\% of the
pixels at \SI{140}{\decibel}. The tile models, which cannot represent the
obstruction of distant sites, predict that \sitesNeedGbdt{} suffices
(GBDT; \sitesNeedOracle{} with the true labels, averages over the cities),

\subsection{Decomposition of the gap and physics baselines}
\label{sec:res-decomp}

The gap between the tile model and link transfer measured above has three
candidate causes: the absence of a reachability model, the absence of a
line-of-sight state, and the per-tile summary of the link geometry itself.
This subsection separates them with the receiver-tile variants of
Section~\ref{sec:robust} and compares the learned link model with the
non-learned per-link baselines of Section~\ref{sec:link}, which use the
same geometry without training.

Table~\ref{tab:variants} and Fig.~\ref{fig:decomp} attribute the gap.
Giving GBDT tile transfer the hurdle of the link model leaves its
path-loss error over the ray-traced links at \SI{\varGbdtHNet}{\decibel},
because the hurdle removes links without changing the loss predicted for
the others, but lowers its censored error from
\SI{\varGbdtCens}{\decibel} to \SI{\varGbdtHCens}{\decibel} and its planning
regret from \varGbdtRegret\% to \varGbdtHRegret\% (\dDecHurdleRegret{}
percentage points, [\loDecHurdleRegret, \hiDecHurdleRegret]): the cut-off,
not the path-loss accuracy, is what makes the plans of the tile models
unreliable. The dual-slope label of Section~\ref{sec:link}, which gives every
tile a line-of-sight and an obstructed pair (Table~\ref{tab:los}:
\losShareMin--\losShareMax\% of the links are in line of sight; median
exponents \losGammaMedian{} and \nlosGammaMedian), lifts the nearest-site
restriction and changes the serving-site accuracy of GBDT from
\varGbdtAssoc\% to \varGbdtLosAssoc\% (\dDecLosAssoc{} points,
[\loDecLosAssoc, \hiDecLosAssoc]) and the path-loss error to
\SI{\varGbdtLosNet}{\decibel}; with both, the tile model reaches
\SI{\varGbdtLosHNet}{\decibel}, \varGbdtLosHAssoc\% and a regret of
\varGbdtLosHRegret\%. With exact labels the same two additions bring the
tile model to \SI{\varOracleLosHNet}{\decibel}, \varOracleLosHAssoc\% and
\varOracleLosHRegret\%. Link transfer remains better than the best tile
variant with transferred labels (\varBestTileNetProse{} in path loss) in
\nLinkBetterTileNet{} of \nVarFolds{} cities in path loss, in
\nLinkBetterTileAssoc{} in serving site and in \nLinkBetterTileRegret{} in
regret (\dDecLinkBestTile{} dB, [\loDecLinkBestTile, \hiDecLinkBestTile]),
and than the dual-slope hurdle model with \emph{exact} labels by
\dDecLinkOracleLosH{} dB ([\loDecLinkOracleLosH, \hiDecLinkOracleLosH]) and
\dDecLinkOracleLosHAssoc{} points of serving site. What remains after the
cut-off and the line-of-sight state is the geometry of the individual link:
how far above the line of sight the obstruction rises, how many buildings
the path crosses and how long it runs over them, which no per-tile summary
carries.

The non-learned per-link baselines use exactly that geometry. Free space
with a single knife edge, Eq.~\eqref{eq:knife}, gives
\SI{\varKeNet}{\decibel}, the Deygout construction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:deygout}
\SI{\varDeygoutNet}{\decibel} and 3GPP UMa with the geometric
line-of-sight state, Eq.~\eqref{eq:uma}, \SI{\varUmaGeoNet}{\decibel}; the
best of them is \varBestPhysNetProse{} in path loss (\varBestPhysNetVal{}\,dB),
\varBestPhysAssocProse{} in serving site (\varBestPhysAssocVal\%) and
\varBestPhysRegretProse{} in planning regret (\varBestPhysRegretVal\%). Link
transfer is better than the best physics baseline in
\nLinkBetterPhysNet{} of \nVarFolds{} cities in path loss, in
\nLinkBetterPhysAssoc{} in serving site and in \nLinkBetterPhysRegret{} in
regret; against the Deygout model the differences are \dDecLinkDeygout{} dB
([\loDecLinkDeygout, \hiDecLinkDeygout]), \dDecLinkDeygoutAssoc{} points of
serving site and \dDecLinkDeygoutRegret{} points of regret, and against UMa
with geometric line of sight \dDecLinkUma{} dB ([\loDecLinkUma,
\hiDecLinkUma]) and \dDecLinkUmaAssoc{} points. The planning regret is the
one quantity in which a physics baseline is as good: the Deygout
construction with the deterministic hurdle plans five sites with a regret of
\varDeygoutRegret\% against \regretLink\% for link transfer, although its
path loss is in error by \SI{\varDeygoutNet}{\decibel}, because a large but
nearly uniform overestimate does not change which sites cover the most
pixels. Of the \nDecompLinkTotal{} comparisons of link transfer with the
tile variants and the physics baselines, \nDecompLinkAllCities{} hold in
every city and \nDecompLinkHolm{} survive Holm's correction. The learned
link model thus adds accuracy in path loss, serving site, SINR and coverage
to the diffraction physics it is built on; a per-link physics model with a
reach model is already enough for a five-site plan on this corpus.

\subsection{Mixed-path composition}
\label{sec:res-mixed}

A tile model need not describe a link by the parameters of the receiver
tile alone: the framework of~\cite{ozyurt2026} composes the loss from every
tile the path crosses, Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixed}, and larger tiles than the
\SI{100}{\metre} of this corpus. This subsection tests whether either
choice closes the gap to link transfer, with exact and with transferred
labels.

Composing the tile parameters along each path lifts the nearest-site
restriction, and with exact labels it helps (Table~\ref{tab:mixed},
Fig.~\ref{fig:mixed}a): the path-averaged composition, fitted to the links
of each city, has a path-loss error of \SI{\mixAvgNetOracle}{\decibel}
against \SI{\netOracle}{\decibel} for the receiver-tile labels, selects the
correct serving site for \mixAvgAssocOracle\% of the pixels against
\assocOracle\%, places the cell edges \SI{\mixAvgEdgeOracle}{\metre} from
the true ones and loses \mixAvgRegretOracle\% of the pixels when planning
five sites. Most of the gain does not survive transfer. With parameters
transferred by GBDT the path-averaged composition has a path-loss error of
\SI{\mixAvgNetGbdt}{\decibel} (receiver-tile transfer:
\SI{\netGbdt}{\decibel}; better in \mixAvgBetterRecvPl{} of \nFolds{}
cities) and a serving-site accuracy of \mixAvgAssocGbdt\%, above the
nearest-site rule in \mixAvgBetterRecvAssoc{} of \nFolds{} cities: every
link now accumulates the transfer errors of all the tiles it crosses, and
these errors decide which site appears strongest. Its planning regret is
\mixAvgRegretGbdt\% against \regretGbdt\%, still far above the
\regretLink\% of link transfer. The piecewise composition gains little over
the receiver tile even with exact labels (\SI{\mixPwNetOracle}{\decibel},
\mixPwAssocOracle\%), and with transferred exponents it is the worse of the
two (GBDT \SI{\mixPwNetGbdt}{\decibel}, \mixPwAssocGbdt\%): an error in the
exponent of one tile shifts the loss of every link that passes through it.

Larger tiles, as in~\cite{ozyurt2026}, do not change the picture
(Table~\ref{tab:mixedsize}, Fig.~\ref{fig:mixed}b): with tiles of
\SI{200}{\metre} to \SI{1000}{\metre} the path-averaged and piecewise
compositions have errors of \mixSizeAvgOracleMin{} to
\SI{\mixSizeAvgOracleMax}{\decibel} and \mixSizePwOracleMin{} to
\SI{\mixSizePwOracleMax}{\decibel} with exact labels, and
\mixSizeAvgGbdtMin{} to \SI{\mixSizeAvgGbdtMax}{\decibel} and
\mixSizePwGbdtMin{} to \SI{\mixSizePwGbdtMax}{\decibel} with GBDT. Over
all compositions and tile sizes, the best tile model with exact labels
reaches \SI{\mixSizeBestNet}{\decibel} and \mixSizeBestAssoc\% of the
serving sites, and link transfer is \mixSizeLinkBetterBest{} than it in
both; against the path-averaged composition with exact labels, link
transfer is better in \mixAvgLinkBetterPl{} of \nFolds{} cities in path
loss and in \mixAvgLinkBetterAssoc{} in serving site, and at least as good
in planning regret in \mixAvgLinkBetterRegret. The limitation lies in
describing a link by tile parameters, however they are combined and
whatever the tile size.

For completeness, the summed reading of the composition is also reported.
Algorithm~3 of~\cite{ozyurt2026} accumulates, for every tile a path
crosses, a term $A_t+10\gamma_t\log_{10}L_t$ with $L_t$ the length of the
path inside the tile in metres and $A_t$ the intercept of the tile at
\SI{1}{\metre}. Read literally as a propagation law, the sum adds one
intercept per crossed tile, so the loss grows with the number of tiles
crossed rather than with the logarithm of the distance. With parameters
fitted to the links of each city by the same joint least squares as the
other two readings, its fit residual is \mixSumFitMin{} to
\SI{\mixSumFitMax}{\decibel} and the transferred parameters give
\mixSumTransMin{} to \SI{\mixSumTransMax}{\decibel} (Table~\ref{tab:mixed};
\mixSizeSumOracleMin{} to \SI{\mixSizeSumOracleMax}{\decibel} with exact
labels at the tile sizes of Table~\ref{tab:mixedsize}). The published
framework was evaluated against measurements by the normalised error of the
received power and may normalise the accumulated terms in a way its text
does not state; the piecewise reading, which the same accumulation defines
once the intercept is counted once, is treated here as its faithful form,
and no conclusion about the framework is drawn from the summed reading.

\subsection{Survey design and certified coverage}
\label{sec:res-survey}
\label{sec:res-certified}

Two instruments of Section~\ref{sec:methods} connect the link model to the
operation of a digital twin: the calibration of its per-site offsets from a
small survey of the target city, Eq.~\eqref{eq:offset}, and the city-level
coverage certificate of Proposition~\ref{prop:certify}. Both are evaluated
here on the held-out cities; the survey is revisited on the external scenes
in Section~\ref{sec:res-robust}, where it matters most.

Calibrating the per-site offsets of the link model from surveyed tiles
changes it little on the corpus (Table~\ref{tab:survey},
Fig.~\ref{fig:survey}, scored on the unsurveyed pixels). With 25 random
tiles and the empirical-Bayes $\kappa$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:offset} the
path-loss error goes from \SI{\svPlZero}{\decibel} to
\SI{\svPlRTwentyFive}{\decibel} (\dNetLinkTwentyFive{} dB,
[\loNetLinkTwentyFive, \hiNetLinkTwentyFive], better in
\nNetLinkTwentyFive{} of \nFolds{} cities) and the serving-site accuracy
from \svAssocZero\% to \svAssocRTwentyFive\%, with the regret unchanged at
\svRegRTwentyFive\%; the estimated $\kappa$ has a median of
\kappaMedianTwentyFive{} links and reaches its ceiling, which switches the
per-site offsets off, in \kappaShareLargeTwentyFive\% of the draws. The
fixed $\kappa=\kappaLink{}$ links gives \SI{\svPlFTwentyFive}{\decibel}
(\dNetFixedKappa{} dB against the empirical-Bayes value, [\loNetFixedKappa,
\hiNetFixedKappa]). The variance design of Proposition~\ref{prop:survey}
gives \SI{\svPlDTwentyFive}{\decibel} (\dNetDesignVar{} dB against random
tiles, [\loNetDesignVar, \hiNetDesignVar]) and the stratified design
\SI{\svPlSTwentyFive}{\decibel} (\dNetDesignStrat{} dB, [\loNetDesignStrat,
\hiNetDesignStrat]); the best of the four variants at $k=25$ is
\svBestTwentyFive. The zero-shot link model has little systematic per-site
error on this corpus for a survey of this size to remove, and the variance
design, which favours tiles seen by many sites rather than tiles
representative of the city, has the largest error of the four.

\subsection{Robustness}
\label{sec:res-robust}

The main results rest on one tile size, one cut-off, one network density,
one siting rule, one material model and one band. This subsection varies
each of them in turn, following Section~\ref{sec:robust}, and closes with
the external scenes, which were modelled independently of the corpus.

Table~\ref{tab:tilesize} repeats the tile pipeline for
$G_s\in\{50,100,200\}$\,m; $G_s=\SI{100}{\metre}$ reproduces the main
results exactly. Larger tiles give more precise labels (cluster-robust
standard error \tsSeFifty, \tsSeHundred{} and \tsSeTwoHundred) and a smaller
exponent error (GBDT \tsGammaFifty, \tsGammaHundred{} and \tsGammaTwoHundred),
but a coarser map: the path-loss error of the oracle goes from
\SI{\tsOracleFifty}{\decibel} to \SI{\tsOracleTwoHundred}{\decibel}, and that
of GBDT tile transfer from \SI{\tsGbdtFifty}{\decibel} to
\SI{\tsGbdtTwoHundred}{\decibel}. The serving-site accuracy of the tile model
is \tsAssocRange\% at every size, as the nearest-site rule implies. No tile
size approaches link transfer. The labels also depend on the dynamic range
of the data (Table~\ref{tab:censoring}). Between \censMin\% and \censMax\%
of the (pixel, site) pairs of the labelled tiles lie beyond the cut-off;
lowering the cut-off to \SI{140}{\decibel} changes the median tile exponent
by \censDgOneForty{} and to \SI{130}{\decibel} by \censDgOneThirty, and a
Tobit fit that also uses the censored pairs gives slopes larger than the
least-squares slopes by a median of \censTobitMin{} to \censTobitMax{}
across the cities (\censTobitTiles{} tiles). The tile exponent thus
depends on the dynamic range of the measurements, not only on the
environment, which is a further reason to model links, and to model the
cut-off explicitly as the hurdle of the link model does. On the network of
the first \nSitesSmall{} sites (Table~\ref{tab:density}), the ranking is
unchanged: the oracle has a path-loss error of
\SI{\smallNetOracle}{\decibel}, GBDT tile transfer
\SI{\smallNetGbdt}{\decibel} and link transfer \SI{\smallNetLink}{\decibel},
with serving-site accuracies of \smallAssocOracle\%, \smallAssocGbdt\% and
\smallAssocLink\%.
