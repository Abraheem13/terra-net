\begin{abstract}
Network digital twins are expected to become a standard component of the
planning and operation of sixth-generation (6G) mobile networks, and they
require a propagation model for areas where no measurement campaign exists
yet. Tile-based transfer fits a log-distance exponent and intercept per map
tile and carries them to unmeasured tiles by descriptor similarity. This
article evaluates such transfer across cities, and measures what its errors
cost a network planner, on open ray-traced corpora: \nCities{}
geo-referenced cities with \nSites{} rooftop sites at
\SI{\freqGHz}{\giga\hertz} and in the upper mid-band at
\SI{\freqTwoGHz}{\giga\hertz} (\nSamples{} and \nSamplesTwo{} links),
replicates with other site placements and random building materials, and
independently modelled external scenes. The tile labels are shown to be
imprecise, with a cluster-robust uncertainty \seRatio{} times the naive one,
and to change with the serving sites, the tile size and the dynamic range of
the data. At the network level, even exact tile labels leave a path-loss
error of \SI{\netOracle}{\decibel} and assign the correct serving site to
\assocOracle\% of the locations. The gap is decomposed: a reachability model
for distant sites removes most of the planning loss, a line-of-sight
dual-slope label part of the remainder, and the rest is the geometry of each
link. A site-aware hurdle model, which predicts every link and whether it
exists from its obstruction and diffraction geometry, reaches
\SI{\netLink}{\decibel}, \assocLink\% correct serving sites and a planning
regret of \regretLink\% for a five-site plan in every held-out city, and
outperforms per-link physics baselines in path loss and serving site under
the same protocol. On scenes with other materials, a survey of 25 random
tiles calibrates it to the accuracy of exact tile labels, and a
distribution-free city-level certificate allows \certClaimLink\% of the
locations to be promised as covered at a guaranteed risk, against
\certClaimGbdt\% for tile transfer. All corpora, code and results are
released, and every number, table and figure of the article is regenerated
by one command.
\end{abstract}
