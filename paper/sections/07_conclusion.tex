\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We built reproducible ray-traced corpora of \nCities{} cities in two bands,
with siting and material replicates, for cross-city transfer of path-loss
models in 6G network digital twins, and evaluated transfer where a network
planner uses it. Tile labels of the log-distance law are ill-conditioned
and, once their correlated errors are accounted for, imprecise: the
cluster-robust standard error of the exponent is \seRatio{} times the naive
one, and the exponent depends on the serving sites, the tile size and the
cut-off of the data. Transferring tile parameters with learned regressors
improves on the source median in \nGbdtMedian{} of \nFolds{} cities,
although not significantly after correction for multiple comparisons;
kernel transfer improves on it less, and the analysis of its limiting
regimes and the gain from learning its metric locate its weakness in the
isotropic distance. At the network level, even perfect tile parameters
leave a path-loss error of \SI{\netOracle}{\decibel}, assign only
\assocOracle\% of the pixels to the correct site and misplace cell edges by
\SI{\edgeOracle}{\metre}. A hurdle for the cut-off removes most of the
planning loss of the tile model and a line-of-sight dual-slope label part
of the association error; the remainder is the geometry of each link.
Site-aware hurdle link transfer, which predicts each link and whether it
exists from its obstruction geometry, reduces the path-loss error to
\SI{\netLink}{\decibel}, raises serving-site accuracy to \assocLink\% and
lowers the planning regret in every held-out city, outperforms the per-link
diffraction and 3GPP baselines built on the same geometry, and a city-level
certificate turns its accuracy into coverage that can be promised at a
stated risk.

Future work should validate these findings on measured data with known site
positions, extend the corpora to more cities, terrain, vegetation and
antenna patterns, and study certificates that adapt to the target city from
a small survey.
