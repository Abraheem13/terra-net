\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methods}

This section describes how the corpora are built, how the tile labels and
descriptors are obtained, which transfer operators and link models are
compared, and how every predicted map is evaluated. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}
places the evaluated components in the digital twin network architecture
of ITU-T Y.3090~\cite{ituy3090}: the data repository holds the building
data, the city scenes, the ray-traced corpora and the tile table; the
service mapping models are the tile transfer operators, their few-shot
correction, the site-aware link model and the per-link physics baselines;
and the network applications derive coverage, cell edges, site plans and
coverage certificates from the predicted path loss. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}
gives the processing order and Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol} the nested
leave-one-city-out protocol under which every model is fitted and scored.
Every stage is implemented in the accompanying repository and executed by a
single command, and every number reported in
Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:discussion} is generated from its
outputs.

\subsection{Ray-traced corpora}
\label{sec:corpus}

The corpora are the ground truth of the study and are built so that every
step from the building data to the links can be reproduced exactly. Each
city is a square of side \SI{\sceneSize}{\kilo\metre} centred on a fixed
coordinate and expressed in a local metric frame, the UTM projection of its
zone shifted to the centre~\cite{snyder1987}. Building footprints are taken
from the buildings theme of Overture Maps~\cite{overture}, release
\overtureRelease{}, a versioned open dataset whose primary source is
OpenStreetMap~\cite{haklay2008osm}; a pinned release makes the geometry
exactly reproducible, which a live map query is not. Because grid north and
true north differ, the footprint query box covers all four corners of the
square. Building heights are resolved in order from the height attribute,
from the number of floors at \SI{3.2}{\metre} per floor, and otherwise from
the median of the resolved heights of the city; the share of each source is
reported per city. Footprint exteriors are clipped to the square, parts
smaller than \SI{4}{\metre\squared} are discarded, and the remainder is
extruded to level-of-detail~1 and placed on a flat ground plane. Buildings
use the ITU concrete model and the ground the ITU medium-dry-ground
model~\cite{itup2040}.

Each city has \nSitesPerCity{} sites on rooftops: local height maxima of
the building mesh on a \SI{25}{\metre} grid are ranked by height and
selected greedily with a minimum separation of \SI{\minSep}{\metre}, and the
antenna is mounted \SI{\siteMast}{\metre} above the roof; if too few
candidates satisfy the separation, the remaining sites are drawn at random
from the other rooftop candidates with a fixed seed. Ranking rooftops by
height follows macro-cell practice but places the sites of high-rise cities
on their tallest buildings: the median site height per city ranges from
\SI{\siteHMedMin}{\metre} (\siteHMedMinCity) to \SI{\siteHMedMax}{\metre}
(\siteHMedMaxCity), with a maximum of \SI{\siteHMax}{\metre} in
\siteHMaxCity{} (Table~\ref{tab:corpus}). Radio maps are computed with
Sionna~RT~\cite{hoydis2023sionna,hoydis2024learning} (version
\sionnaVersion) at \SI{\freqGHz}{\giga\hertz} with isotropic antennas,
\rtSamples{} rays per site, maximum interaction depth \rtDepth{}, specular
and diffuse reflection, refraction and diffraction (including edge
diffraction) enabled, on a \SI{\cellSize}{\metre} grid at
\SI{\ueHeight}{\metre} receiver height. Cells with path loss above
$L_{\max}=\SI{\maxPl}{\decibel}$, beyond any practical coupling loss, are
discarded; a cell that no site reaches below $L_{\max}$ is a coverage hole
of the truth and is kept as such in the evaluation. The solver version and
settings are stored with every city. \nSitesWord{} sites on
\SI{\sceneArea}{\kilo\metre\squared} give one site per
\SI{\areaPerSite}{\kilo\metre\squared}, the density of a hexagonal layout
with an inter-site distance of about \SI{\isdMain}{\metre}, sparser than the
\SI{500}{\metre} of the 3GPP urban-macro scenario~\cite{tr38901}. The
density and the siting rule are tested in Section~\ref{sec:res-robust}.

Four further corpora use the same scenes. The \emph{second band} re-traces
every site at \SI{\freqTwoGHz}{\giga\hertz}, in the \SIrange{7}{24}{\giga\hertz}
range studied for 6G~\cite{tr38820}. The \emph{random-siting replicate}
re-traces \nRandCitiesWord{} cities (\randCities) at \SI{\freqGHz}{\giga\hertz}
with the same number of sites taken from the rooftop candidates in random
order instead of by height, under the same separation rule; the
\emph{mid-rise replicate} draws them in random order from the rooftops
between \SI{\cappedLo}{\metre} and \SI{\cappedHi}{\metre} above ground, the
site-height range of the 3GPP urban-macro scenario; and the \emph{materials
replicate} keeps the main siting but assigns each building part one of
\nMatWord{} ITU materials (\matList) at random with a fixed seed instead of
concrete for all. The geometry, and hence every descriptor and link
feature, is identical across these replicates; only the ray-traced truth
changes.

The planar radio-map estimator of the solver weights a ray crossing the
measurement plane by the inverse cosine of its incidence angle, and a
diffracted ray by an integration weight over the diffraction cone; the
first weight is unbounded and the second can be very large, so a cell
occasionally receives one overweighted contribution. Its path loss can then
fall below free space by tens of decibels, which no incoherent sum of a few
propagation paths allows. With the free-space loss
\begin{equation}
P_{L,\mathrm{FS}}(d)=20\log_{10}\frac{4\pi d f}{c_0}
\label{eq:fspl}
\end{equation}
at carrier frequency $f$ and speed of light $c_0$, every link with
$\PL(p,b)<P_{L,\mathrm{FS}}(d_{pb})-\SI{\qcMargin}{\decibel}$ is removed:
\qcRemoved{} links, \qcShare\% of the corpus. For the site with the most
such links, disabling diffraction reduces the links more than
\SI{\qcMargin}{\decibel} below free space from \outDiffOn{} to \outDiffOff{}
with otherwise identical settings, which identifies the diffraction weights
as their source.

\subsection{Tile labels and descriptors}
\label{sec:labels}

The labels are the quantity that tile transfer carries from city to city,
and their precision bounds what any operator can achieve; this subsection
therefore derives the estimator and three estimates of its error. Links are
assigned to tiles of side $G_s=\SI{\tileSize}{\metre}$ by their receiver
position; tiles with fewer than \minLinks{} links are not labelled. With
$n_t$ links in tile $t$, centred regressor $x_i-\bar x_t$ and the sums
$S_{xx,t}=\sum_i(x_i-\bar x_t)^{2}$ and $S_{xy,t}=\sum_i(x_i-\bar x_t)(y_i-\bar y_t)$,
the ordinary least-squares (OLS) slope and its variance under independent
residuals of variance $s_t^{2}$ are
\begin{equation}
b_t=\frac{S_{xy,t}}{S_{xx,t}},\qquad
v_t^{\mathrm{iid}}=\frac{s_t^{2}}{S_{xx,t}}=\frac{s_t^{2}}{n_t\Var_t(x)} .
\label{eq:ols}
\end{equation}
The slope is poorly determined because every receiver sees the serving sites
from a similar distance: a receiver at distance $d$ from its site sees a tile
of side $G_s$ span only $\Delta x\approx10\log_{10}(1+G_s/d)$, which is
\SI{0.41}{\decibel} for $G_s=\SI{100}{\metre}$ and $d=\SI{1}{\kilo\metre}$,
so $\Var_t(x)$ vanishes and $v_t^{\mathrm{iid}}$ diverges. The slope is
therefore shrunk towards the city-wide mean with weights set by the data
(empirical Bayes~\cite{morris1983}).

\begin{proposition}[Empirical-Bayes shrinkage]
\label{prop:eb}
Let $b_t$ be the OLS slope of tile $t$, computed with $x$ centred inside
the tile, with sampling variance $v_t$, and let the true slopes vary between
tiles with mean $\mu$ and variance $\tau^{2}$. Among estimators
$\hat\gamma_t=(1-w)b_t+w\mu$ the mean squared error $(1-w)^{2}v_t+w^{2}\tau^{2}$
is minimised by
\begin{equation}
w_t=\frac{v_t}{v_t+\tau^{2}}=\frac{\lambda_t}{\lambda_t+S_{xx,t}},
\qquad \lambda_t=\frac{S_{xx,t}\,v_t}{\tau^{2}},
\label{eq:eb}
\end{equation}
which is ridge regression on the centred slope with penalty $\lambda_t$
and prior mean $\mu$. Its risk is $v_t\tau^{2}/(v_t+\tau^{2})\le\min(v_t,\tau^{2})$,
bounded by $\tau^{2}$ however degenerate the tile geometry.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Given the true slope, $b_t$ has mean $\gamma_t$ and variance $v_t$, and the
true slopes have mean $\mu$ and variance $\tau^{2}$. For fixed $w$,
$\hat\gamma_t-\gamma_t=(1-w)(b_t-\gamma_t)+w(\mu-\gamma_t)$; the two terms
are uncorrelated, so the mean squared error is $(1-w)^{2}v_t+w^{2}\tau^{2}$,
a convex quadratic in $w$ minimised at $w_t=v_t/(v_t+\tau^{2})$ with value
$v_t\tau^{2}/(v_t+\tau^{2})$, which is at most $\min(v_t,\tau^{2})$. With
centred $x$, the ridge problem
$\min_\gamma\sum_i(y_i-\bar y-\gamma x_i)^{2}+\lambda(\gamma-\mu)^{2}$ has
solution $(S_{xy}+\lambda\mu)/(S_{xx}+\lambda)=(1-w)b_t+w\mu$ with
$w=\lambda/(\lambda+S_{xx})$; setting $\lambda_t=S_{xx}v_t/\tau^{2}$ gives
$w=w_t$.
\end{proof}

\noindent The prior parameters $(\mu,\tau^{2})$ are estimated per city
from the tiles with a non-degenerate span by the DerSimonian--Laird moment
estimator~\cite{dersimonian1986}: with $\omega_t=1/v_t$, the fixed-effect
mean $\mu_{\mathrm{FE}}=\sum_t\omega_t b_t/\sum_t\omega_t$ and the
heterogeneity statistic $Q=\sum_t\omega_t(b_t-\mu_{\mathrm{FE}})^{2}$ over
$m$ tiles,
\begin{equation}
\hat\tau^{2}=\max\Bigl\{0,\;\frac{Q-(m-1)}{\sum_t\omega_t-\sum_t\omega_t^{2}/\sum_t\omega_t}\Bigr\},
\qquad
\hat\mu=\frac{\sum_t b_t/(v_t+\hat\tau^{2})}{\sum_t 1/(v_t+\hat\tau^{2})} .
\label{eq:dl}
\end{equation}
The label is then $\hat\gamma_t=(1-w_t)b_t+w_t\hat\mu$ with the intercept
left unpenalised, $\hat P_{L_0,t}=\bar y_t-\hat\gamma_t\bar x_t$; a tile with
no span takes $\hat\gamma_t=\hat\mu$. The shrinkage weight $w_t$ is
published with every label. Algorithm~\ref{alg:labels} states the
procedure; it is vectorised over all links of a city.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\SetAlgoLined\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{links $(x_i,y_i,t_i,b_i)$ of one city; $n_{\min}$}
\KwOut{$\hat\btheta_t$, $w_t$, iid and cluster-robust standard errors}
\ForEach{tile $t$ with $n_t\ge n_{\min}$}{
  compute $\bar x_t$, $\bar y_t$, $S_{xx,t}$ and $S_{xy,t}$\;
  $b_t\leftarrow S_{xy,t}/S_{xx,t}$\;
  $v_t\leftarrow$ cluster-robust variance of $b_t$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:cr}\;
}
$(\hat\mu,\hat\tau^{2})\leftarrow$ DerSimonian--Laird over $\{(b_t,v_t)\}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:dl}\;
\ForEach{tile $t$}{
  $w_t\leftarrow v_t/(v_t+\hat\tau^{2})$\;
  $\hat\gamma_t\leftarrow(1-w_t)b_t+w_t\hat\mu$\;
  $\hat P_{L_0,t}\leftarrow\bar y_t-\hat\gamma_t\bar x_t$\;
  $e_i\leftarrow y_i-\hat P_{L_0,t}-\hat\gamma_t x_i$ for the links of $t$\;
  standard errors from Eq.~\eqref{eq:cr}, with iid and with site clusters\;
}
\caption{Tile labels}
\label{alg:labels}
\end{algorithm}

Radio-map cells served by the same site share propagation paths, so the
independent-residual variance of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ols} understates $v_t$. The
estimator is linear in the path losses: in the centred parametrisation
$y_i=a_t+\gamma_t(x_i-\bar x_t)+e_i$ with design rows
$\mathbf{z}_i=(1,x_i-\bar x_t)$, the coefficients are
$\mathbf{M}\sum_i\mathbf{z}_i y_i$ up to the prior term, with
$\mathbf{M}=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(1/n_t,\,1/(S_{xx,t}+\lambda_t)\bigr)$
($\lambda_t=0$ for the OLS slope). With the links of the tile partitioned by
serving site into $G$ clusters $\mathcal{I}_1,\dots,\mathcal{I}_G$, with
design blocks $\mathbf{Z}_c$ and residuals $\mathbf{e}_c$, the
cluster-robust variance is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Var}_{\mathrm{cl}}=\mathbf{M}\Bigl(\sum_{c=1}^{G}\mathbf{u}_c\mathbf{u}_c^{\top}\Bigr)\mathbf{M},
\quad
\mathbf{u}_c=\mathbf{Z}_c^{\top}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}_{cc})^{-1/2}\mathbf{e}_c,
\label{eq:cr}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{H}_{cc}=\mathbf{Z}_c\mathbf{M}\mathbf{Z}_c^{\top}$ (Moore--Penrose
inverse root where $\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}_{cc}$ is singular). This is the
bias-reduced linearisation (CR2) of~\cite{bell2002,pustejovsky2018}; without
the factor $(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}_{cc})^{-1/2}$ it is the usual
estimator~\cite{liang1986,cameron2015}, which is biased downwards when the
clusters are few, as here. Since
$\mathbf{Z}_c^{\top}f(\mathbf{Z}_c\mathbf{M}\mathbf{Z}_c^{\top})=f(\mathbf{Z}_c^{\top}\mathbf{Z}_c\mathbf{M})\mathbf{Z}_c^{\top}$
for $f(s)=(1-s)^{-1/2}$, every $\mathbf{u}_c$ requires only the
eigen-decomposition of a $2\times2$ matrix. The cluster-robust variance of
the slope is used as $v_t$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:eb}, never below the
independent value; a tile served by a single site uses the independent
value multiplied by the median ratio of the two over the city (its design
effect). Because the number of clusters per tile is small,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cr} is complemented by a split-half estimate: the sites of a
city are divided at random into two disjoint halves, the complete estimator
(including its own $\hat\mu,\hat\tau^2$) is refitted on each half, and
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{split}}(\hat\gamma_t)=
\frac{\operatorname{sd}\bigl(\hat\gamma_t^{(1)}-\hat\gamma_t^{(2)}\bigr)}{2}
\label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
estimates the standard error of the full-data label: the standard
deviation of the difference divided by $\sqrt2$ is the error of a half-data
label, and a further division by $\sqrt2$ extrapolates to the full data
under the assumption that the variance scales with the inverse number of
sites. Five random partitions are pooled.

The descriptor must describe exactly the geometry the ray tracer saw. It is
therefore computed from the same footprint parts, heights and scene clipping
as the extruded mesh, with every part clipped to the tile it falls in. It
contains the $32$ built-environment features of~\cite{ozyurt2026}: footprint
ratio and building count in five height classes ($<5$, $5$--$10$,
$10$--$30$, $30$--$60$, $\ge\SI{60}{\metre}$); mean and standard deviation of
footprint area, height and volume; total footprint density; and, for
horizontal planes at $5$, $10$, $30$, $60$ and \SI{100}{\metre}, the number
of buildings reaching the plane and the mean and standard deviation of the
distance between their centroids. The topographic, land-cover and climatic
descriptors of~\cite{ozyurt2026} are omitted on purpose: the scenes have a
flat ground, no water or vegetation surfaces and no weather, so these
features would describe nothing the solver modelled and could only act as
city identifiers. Descriptors are stored unnormalised; every fit scales
them by the column-wise maximum absolute value of its own training tiles,
\begin{equation}
\tilde v_{t,\ell}=\frac{v_{t,\ell}}{\max_{j\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{S}}}|v_{j,\ell}|},
\qquad \ell=1,\dots,p,
\label{eq:scale}
\end{equation}
never of the target.

\subsection{Transfer operators and nested selection}
\label{sec:operators}

Thirteen operators are compared under one protocol: constant predictors,
label-averaging operators and learned regressors, with every
hyper-parameter chosen inside the training cities. Their prediction rules
are the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Source median}: the median source label, the no-information
reference.
\item \emph{3GPP UMa}: the line-of-sight and non-line-of-sight single-slope
formulas of TR~38.901~\cite{tr38901} at the reference distance, which give
fixed parameters for every tile, and \emph{COST-231 Hata}~\cite{cost231}
(metropolitan correction, median site height of the source cities), used
outside its validity range of \SIrange{1.5}{2}{\giga\hertz} and
\SIrange{1}{20}{\kilo\metre} as a further standard model.
\item \emph{Kernel (Nadaraya--Watson)}~\cite{nadaraya1964,watson1964}, the
operator of~\cite{ozyurt2026}:
\begin{equation}
\hat\btheta_u=\sum_j\tilde w_j(u)\btheta_j,\qquad
\tilde w_j(u)\propto\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\lVert\bv_u-\bv_j\rVert^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\Bigr),
\label{eq:nw}
\end{equation}
computed with the minimum squared distance subtracted in the exponent, which
leaves the weights unchanged and avoids numerical underflow.
\item \emph{GP-ARD}: a Gaussian process with one length scale per
descriptor (automatic relevance determination)~\cite{rasmussen2006}, i.e.\ a
kernel operator whose metric is learned. With the kernel
\begin{equation}
k(\bv,\bv')=\sigma_f^{2}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac12\sum_{\ell=1}^{p}\frac{(v_\ell-v'_\ell)^{2}}{\ell_\ell^{2}}\Bigr)
+\sigma_n^{2}\,\ind\{\bv=\bv'\},
\label{eq:ard}
\end{equation}
the posterior mean of each standardised target is
$\hat\theta_u=\mathbf{k}_u^{\top}\mathbf{K}^{-1}\mathbf{z}$, where
$\mathbf{K}$ is the kernel matrix of the source tiles, $\mathbf{k}_u$ the
vector of kernel values between $\bv_u$ and the source tiles and
$\mathbf{z}$ the standardised source labels; the hyper-parameters
$(\sigma_f,\ell_1,\dots,\ell_p,\sigma_n)$ maximise the marginal likelihood
on \num{600} random source tiles and the posterior mean uses every source
tile.
\item \emph{$k$-NN}: the mean label of the $k$ nearest source tiles,
$\hat\btheta_u=k^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_k(u)}\btheta_j$.
\item \emph{Ridge}: with $\mathbf{V}$ the $N\times p$ matrix of scaled
source descriptors and $\boldsymbol\Theta$ the $N\times2$ matrix of labels,
\begin{equation}
\hat\btheta_u^{\top}=\tilde{\bv}_u^{\top}\bigl(\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{V}+\alpha\mathbf{I}\bigr)^{-1}\mathbf{V}^{\top}\boldsymbol\Theta,
\label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}
with an unpenalised intercept; a two-layer \emph{MLP} (two hidden layers
of 256 units, targets standardised on the training tiles, trained epoch by
epoch); gradient-boosted trees (\emph{GBDT}, LightGBM~\cite{ke2017lightgbm})
with one model per target, each an additive ensemble
$F_M(\bv)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}f_m(\bv)$ of regression trees fitted by gradient
boosting of the squared error; and a \emph{neural encoder} with a
constrained $(\gamma,P_{L_0})$ head trained with a heteroscedastic Gaussian
likelihood.
\item Two domain-adaptation variants that use the unlabelled descriptors of
the target city. \emph{GBDT-IW} weights the source tiles by the density
ratio of target to source descriptors estimated with a logistic domain
classifier~\cite{shimodaira2000}: with $\pi_j$ the classifier's probability
that source tile $j$ belongs to the target and $N_{\mathrm{T}}$ target
tiles,
\begin{equation}
w_j=\min\Bigl\{\frac{\pi_j}{1-\pi_j}\frac{N}{N_{\mathrm{T}}},\;q_{0.99}\Bigr\},
\label{eq:iw}
\end{equation}
clipped at the 99th percentile $q_{0.99}$ of the weights and normalised to
unit mean. \emph{CORAL} re-colours the source descriptors with the target
covariance before ridge regression~\cite{sun2016coral},
$\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{S}}\mathbf{C}_{\mathrm{S}}^{-1/2}\mathbf{C}_{\mathrm{T}}^{1/2}$,
with both covariances regularised by a small multiple of the identity.
\end{itemize}

Every hyper-parameter (bandwidth $\sigma$, $k$, ridge, CORAL and MLP
penalties, number of boosting rounds, number of epochs) is chosen inside the
training cities by an inner leave-one-training-city-out loop
(Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}): each training city is held out once, the operator
is fitted on the others and scored by the root-mean-square standardised
error over both parameters,
\begin{equation}
\ell=\Bigl(\frac{1}{2n_{\mathrm{v}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\mathrm{v}}}\sum_{q\in\{\gamma,P_{L_0}\}}
\frac{(\hat\theta_{i,q}-\theta_{i,q})^{2}}{s_q^{2}}\Bigr)^{1/2},
\label{eq:selloss}
\end{equation}
with $s_q$ the standard deviation of target $q$ over the training tiles, so
that the exponent and the intercept count equally, and the grid value with
the smallest mean inner error is kept. The operator is then refitted on all
training cities; for the operators with early stopping, the number of
rounds or epochs of the final fit is the median best iteration over the
inner folds. The two expensive operators, the neural encoder and the link
model of Section~\ref{sec:link}, use \nInnerExpensive{} inner validation
cities drawn with a fixed seed. No city is ever reserved for validation, so
the procedure is symmetric in the source cities, which the certificate of
Section~\ref{sec:neteval} requires, and the held-out city is never touched.
The chosen values are listed in Table~\ref{tab:hparams}. Stochastic
operators are run with five seeds; the inner selection uses seed 0.

The behaviour of the kernel operator under shift can be characterised
analytically. Write $d_j=\lVert\bv_u-\bv_j\rVert^{2}$,
$\delta_j=d_j-\min_k d_k\in[0,D_u]$, $\bar\btheta=N^{-1}\sum_j\btheta_j$ and
$\sigma_\theta^{2}=N^{-1}\sum_j\lVert\btheta_j-\bar\btheta\rVert^{2}$.

\begin{proposition}[Two regimes of kernel transfer]
\label{prop:regimes}
(a)~If $\epsilon_u=D_u/(2\sigma^{2})<1$, then
\begin{equation}
\hat\btheta_u=\bar\btheta-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\Cov_j(\delta_j,\btheta_j)+O(\epsilon_u^{2}),\quad
\bigl\lVert\hat\btheta_u-\bar\btheta\bigr\rVert\le
\frac{D_u\,\sigma_\theta}{4\sigma^{2}}+O(\epsilon_u^{2}),
\label{eq:flat}
\end{equation}
and $n_{\mathrm{eff}}(u)=N(1+O(\epsilon_u^{2}))$: the operator returns the
source mean up to a first-order correction.
(b)~Let $g_u$ be the gap between the smallest and second-smallest squared
distance. Then the weight of the nearest source tile satisfies
\begin{equation}
\tilde w_{(1)}\ge\bigl(1+(N-1)e^{-g_u/(2\sigma^{2})}\bigr)^{-1},
\label{eq:nn}
\end{equation}
so $\hat\btheta_u$ tends to the label of the nearest source tile when
$g_u\gg2\sigma^{2}\ln N$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a)~Put $\alpha=1/(2\sigma^{2})$. The factor $e^{-\alpha\min_k d_k}$
cancels in Eq.~\eqref{eq:nw}, so
$\tilde w_j=e^{-\alpha\delta_j}/\sum_k e^{-\alpha\delta_k}$ with
$\alpha\delta_j\le\epsilon_u<1$. By Taylor's theorem
$e^{-\alpha\delta_j}=1-\alpha\delta_j+r_j$ with $0\le r_j\le\epsilon_u^{2}/2$,
hence $\tilde w_j=N^{-1}(1-\alpha(\delta_j-\bar\delta))+O(\epsilon_u^{2}/N)$
and $\hat\btheta_u=\bar\btheta-\alpha\Cov_j(\delta_j,\btheta_j)+O(\epsilon_u^{2})$.
By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality
$\lVert\Cov_j(\delta_j,\btheta_j)\rVert\le\sigma_\delta\sigma_\theta$ with
$\sigma_\delta\le D_u/2$, which gives the bound; the expansion of the
weights gives $\sum_j\tilde w_j^{2}=N^{-1}(1+O(\epsilon_u^{2}))$.
(b)~With the nearest tile at index $(1)$, every other weight satisfies
$e^{-\alpha\delta_j}\le e^{-\alpha g_u}$, so
$\tilde w_{(1)}=1/(1+\sum_{j\ne(1)}e^{-\alpha\delta_j})\ge1/(1+(N-1)e^{-\alpha g_u})$.
\end{proof}

\noindent The minimum distance cancels from Eq.~\eqref{eq:nw}; a target
far from every source tile is therefore \emph{not} by itself pushed to
either regime. Which regime applies is decided by the selected bandwidth
relative to the spread $D_u$ and the gap $g_u$; it is measured
(Section~\ref{sec:res-loco}) rather than assumed. Both regimes carry a
failure mode under shift: the flat regime returns the source mean and
ignores the target descriptor; the nearest-neighbour regime copies one
source tile and transmits its label noise undamped
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp} with $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=1$).

\subsection{Few-shot correction and survey design}
\label{sec:fewshot}

When a small survey of the target city is available, the zero-shot
prediction can be corrected; the correction must respect the precision of
the surveyed labels and must not be applied where the inner cities show no
systematic error. With $k$ target tiles surveyed, the zero-shot prediction
$g(\bv)$ is corrected by the residuals $r_i=\btheta_i-g(\bv_i)$ of the $k$
anchors, weighting each anchor by its precision and shrinking the result by
empirical Bayes, per parameter:
\begin{equation}
w_i=\frac{1}{\mathrm{se}_i^{2}+s^{2}},\quad
c=\frac{\sum_i w_i r_i}{\sum_i w_i},\quad
V=\frac{1}{\sum_i w_i},\quad
c_{\mathrm{EB}}=\frac{\tau_b^{2}}{\tau_b^{2}+V}\,c,
\label{eq:fewshot}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{se}_i$ the cluster-robust standard error of the anchor label.
The within-city error variance $s^{2}$ of the operator and the between-city
variance $\tau_b^{2}$ of its zero-shot bias are estimated on the inner
validation cities of the fold, without any target information: with
$\bar e_c$ and $\mathrm{RMSE}_c$ the mean error and root-mean-square error
of the operator on inner city $c$,
\begin{equation}
\hat s^{2}=\operatorname{mean}_c\bigl(\mathrm{RMSE}_c^{2}-\bar e_c^{2}\bigr),\qquad
\hat\tau_b^{2}=\operatorname{Var}_c(\bar e_c).
\label{eq:ebprior}
\end{equation}
When the inner cities show no systematic bias, $\hat\tau_b^{2}\to0$ and the
correction vanishes; when they do, the correction is the precision-weighted
mean residual. It is compared with the plain mean residual (\emph{bias}) and
with a precision-weighted ridge model of the residual on the descriptors
(\emph{residual}),
\begin{equation}
\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}=\argmin_{\boldsymbol\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_i\bigl(r_i-\boldsymbol\beta^{\top}\tilde{\bv}_i\bigr)^{2}+\lambda\lVert\boldsymbol\beta\rVert^{2},
\label{eq:resid}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda=10$ on normalised descriptors. Anchors are drawn uniformly at
random, as the $k$ tiles closest to a random tile (\emph{contiguous}), which
mimics a single drive-test area~\cite{johansson2012mdt}, or by a
\emph{design} that uses no measurement: the $k$ tiles that greedily maximise
the facility-location objective
\begin{equation}
F_{\mathrm{fl}}(T)=\sum_{i\in\text{target}}\max_{j\in T}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\lVert\bv_i-\bv_j\rVert^{2}}{2h^{2}}\Bigr)
\label{eq:facility}
\end{equation}
over the target tiles, with $h$ the median pairwise descriptor distance;
the objective is monotone submodular, so the greedy set is within a factor
$(1-1/e)$ of the optimum~\cite{nemhauser1978,krause2008}. Evaluation is
always on the unsurveyed tiles, anchors are shared by all operators, and
ten random draws are made per $k\in\{3,5,10,25,50,100\}$.

For the link model of Section~\ref{sec:link}, the links observed in $k$
surveyed tiles, which carry their serving site as MDT reports
do~\cite{johansson2012mdt,ts37320}, calibrate a per-site offset. Model the
residual of a link of site $s$ as $o_s+\varepsilon$, with independent site
offsets $o_s$ of prior mean $\bar o$ and variance $\tau_o^{2}$ and link noise of
variance $\sigma_\varepsilon^{2}$, and let $\kappa=\sigma_\varepsilon^{2}/\tau_o^{2}$.
With $n_s$ observed links of site $s$ and mean residual $\bar r_s$, the
posterior mean and variance of $o_s$ are
\begin{equation}
\hat o_s=\frac{n_s\bar r_s+\kappa\bar o}{n_s+\kappa},\qquad
\Var(o_s\mid\text{survey})=\frac{\tau_o^{2}\kappa}{n_s+\kappa},
\label{eq:offset}
\end{equation}
which are used with $\bar o$ the mean residual of all observed links.
The prior strength $\kappa$ is estimated from the surveyed links themselves
(empirical Bayes): $\hat\sigma_\varepsilon^{2}$ is the pooled within-site
variance of the residuals and $\hat\tau_o^{2}$ the DerSimonian--Laird
between-site variance of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dl} applied to the site means with
variances $\hat\sigma_\varepsilon^{2}/n_s$, so that a survey whose site means
scatter no more than their sampling error applies the overall offset only;
a fixed $\kappa=\kappaLink{}$ links is reported as an ablation.

\begin{proposition}[Survey design]
\label{prop:survey}
Let $c_{ts}\ge0$ be the number of links of site $s$ observable in tile $t$
and $n_s(T)=\sum_{t\in T}c_{ts}$ for a set $T$ of surveyed tiles. The total
reduction of posterior variance of the site offsets,
\begin{equation}
F(T)=\tau_o^{2}\sum_{s}\frac{n_s(T)}{n_s(T)+\kappa},
\label{eq:survey}
\end{equation}
is monotone and submodular in $T$, so the greedy choice of $k$ tiles attains
$F(T_{\mathrm{greedy}})\ge(1-1/e)\max_{|T|=k}F(T)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
From Eq.~\eqref{eq:offset}, $\tau_o^{2}-\Var(o_s\mid T)=\tau_o^{2}n_s(T)/(n_s(T)+\kappa)$,
so $F$ is the total variance reduction. Each $n_s(T)$ is modular and
non-decreasing in $T$, and $x\mapsto x/(x+\kappa)$ is non-decreasing and
concave on $x\ge0$; a non-decreasing concave function of a non-negative
modular function is monotone submodular, and a non-negative sum of such
functions is as well. The greedy guarantee is that
of~\cite{nemhauser1978}.
\end{proof}

\noindent Before any measurement $c_{ts}$ is unknown; the \emph{variance
design} uses the number of links of site $s$ in tile $t$ that the hurdle
classifier of the zero-shot link model predicts below the cut-off. Because
maximising $F$ alone favours tiles that see many sites, which need not be
representative of the city, a \emph{stratified design} first partitions the
tiles into $k$ descriptor strata ($k$-means) and takes in each stratum the
tile with the largest marginal gain of $F$. Both are compared with random
surveys for $k\in\{10,25,50\}$ (ten draws). The surveyed pixels keep their
model prediction, and every few-shot metric is computed on the unsurveyed
pixels only.

\subsection{Site-aware link model and per-link baselines}
\label{sec:link}

As a transfer model free of the receiver-tile restrictions, the path loss
of each (pixel, site) link is predicted directly from the geometry along the
link, which is available for a city without any measurement. The building
heights are rasterised at \SI{2}{\metre} and the straight segment from the
site to the pixel is sampled at $96$ interior points, where the building
height $H(s)$ is compared with the line-of-sight height $h_{\mathrm{los}}(s)$.
The link features are the 3-D and horizontal distances, the site height and
the elevation angle; the fraction of the segment with $H>h_{\mathrm{los}}$,
the number of separate obstructions and the length over footprints; the
largest excess height $\max_s(H-h_{\mathrm{los}})$; the single knife-edge
loss of the most obstructing point, which for an edge of height $h$ above
the line of sight at distances $d_1$ and $d_2$ from the two ends is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J(\nu)&=6.9+20\log_{10}\Bigl(\sqrt{(\nu-0.1)^{2}+1}+\nu-0.1\Bigr),\\
\nu&=h\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda}\Bigl(\frac{1}{d_1}+\frac{1}{d_2}\Bigr)},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:knife}
\end{equation}
for $\nu>-0.78$ and zero otherwise~\cite{itup526}; whether the receiver lies
inside a footprint and that building's height; and the built fraction and
mean building height in a \SI{100}{\metre} square window centred on the
receiver and the built fraction in a \SI{200}{\metre} window centred on the
site. The $32$ descriptors of the receiver's tile are appended, giving 46
features in total.

The ray tracer reports only links below the cut-off, so a regressor trained
on them never learns that a distant, obstructed site is not received at all;
it would predict a finite loss for every link and overstate the reach of
every site. A hurdle model is therefore used: a gradient-boosted
classifier~\cite{ke2017lightgbm} of whether a (pixel, site) pair lies below
the cut-off, trained on \num{120000} pairs of each training city (all sites
and the pixels of its labelled tiles, including the pairs beyond the
cut-off), and a gradient-boosted regressor of the path loss, trained on
\num{120000} ray-traced links of each training city. With $\hat\pi(p,b)$
the classifier's probability and $\widehat{\PL}_{\mathrm{reg}}(p,b)$ the
regression, the predicted map is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\PL}(p,b)=\begin{cases}
\widehat{\PL}_{\mathrm{reg}}(p,b), & \hat\pi(p,b)\ge\tfrac12,\\
+\infty, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:hurdle}
\end{equation}
where $+\infty$ denotes zero received power. The number of rounds of both
models follows the nested rule of Section~\ref{sec:operators} (learning rate
$0.1$, $127$ leaves, at most $1500$ rounds, five seeds). The path-loss error
of the link model is that of the regressor over the ray-traced links; the
regressor alone, which keeps every link, is reported as an ablation, and a
censored error that scores every pair with prediction and truth clipped at
$L_{\max}$ is reported as well.

Three non-learned predictors use the same link geometry and the
deterministic hurdle ``predicted loss $\le L_{\max}$''; they answer the
question whether a learned link model is needed at all once the obstruction
geometry is available. The first is free space plus the single knife-edge
loss of Eq.~\eqref{eq:knife}. The second is free space plus the Deygout
three-edge construction of ITU-R P.526~\cite{itup526}: with $\nu_{\mathrm{p}}$
the largest Fresnel parameter along the path (the principal edge) and
$\nu_{1}$, $\nu_{2}$ the largest parameters on the two sub-paths from each
end to the principal edge,
\begin{equation}
P_{L,\mathrm{D}}=P_{L,\mathrm{FS}}(d)+J(\nu_{\mathrm{p}})+\ind\{\nu_{\mathrm{p}}>-0.78\}\bigl[J(\nu_{1})+J(\nu_{2})\bigr].
\label{eq:deygout}
\end{equation}
The third is the 3GPP UMa model of TR~38.901~\cite{tr38901} with the
line-of-sight state of each link decided from the geometry: with $d_{3}$
and $d_{2}$ the 3-D and horizontal distances in metres, $f_c$ in GHz,
$h_{\mathrm{BS}}$ and $h_{\mathrm{UT}}$ the antenna heights and the
breakpoint distance $d_{\mathrm{BP}}'=4(h_{\mathrm{BS}}-1)(h_{\mathrm{UT}}-1)f/c_0$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_{L,\mathrm{LOS}}&=28.0+22\log_{10}d_{3}+20\log_{10}f_c
&& d_{2}\le d_{\mathrm{BP}}',\\
P_{L,\mathrm{LOS}}&=28.0+40\log_{10}d_{3}+20\log_{10}f_c\\
&\quad-9\log_{10}\bigl(d_{\mathrm{BP}}'^{2}+(h_{\mathrm{BS}}-h_{\mathrm{UT}})^{2}\bigr)
&& d_{2}>d_{\mathrm{BP}}',\\
P_{L,\mathrm{NLOS}}&=\max\bigl\{P_{L,\mathrm{LOS}},\;13.54+39.08\log_{10}d_{3}\\
&\qquad\qquad+20\log_{10}f_c-0.6(h_{\mathrm{UT}}-1.5)\bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:uma}
\end{equation}

To attribute the gap between the tile model and link transfer, every tile
operator is also evaluated (i)~with the hurdle of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hurdle}
applied to its map (seed-matched), (ii)~as a dual-slope model with one
$(\gamma,P_{L_0})$ pair per tile fitted to the line-of-sight links and one
to the obstructed links, the state of each (pixel, site) pair decided from
the geometry, each pair falling back to the all-link label where fewer than
\minLinks{} links of its kind exist, and (iii)~with both. GBDT transfers
the four labels of the dual-slope model under the nested rule.

\subsection{Network-level evaluation, planning and certification}
\label{sec:neteval}

The network application layer of Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} is emulated by
scoring every predicted map against the ray-traced truth
through the quantities of Section~\ref{sec:netmodel}. For the held-out
city, each operator's tile parameters and the true site positions define a
predicted path-loss map for every (pixel, site) pair through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:logdist}; the ray-traced map is the truth over every cell of
the solver grid inside a labelled tile, with pairs beyond the cut-off
treated as zero received power and cells no site reaches as outage pixels
(\unreachedShare\% of the pixels, \unreachedMin--\unreachedMax\% across
cities). The link budget uses $P_{\mathrm{tx}}=\SI{46}{dBm}$,
\SI{20}{\mega\hertz} bandwidth and a \SI{7}{\decibel} noise figure
($P_{\mathrm{N}}=\SI{-94.0}{dBm}$). Let $\mathcal{L}=\{(p,b):\PL(p,b)\le L_{\max}\}$
be the set of ray-traced links, $\widehat{\mathcal{L}}$ the set of pairs the
model predicts below the cut-off, $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{r}}$ the pixels the
truth reaches and $\hat s(p)$ the predicted serving site ($\hat s(p)=\varnothing$
where the model predicts no reaching site). The path-loss metrics are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{RMSE}&=\Bigl(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{L}|}\sum_{(p,b)\in\mathcal{L}}\bigl(\widehat{\PL}(p,b)-\PL(p,b)\bigr)^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2},\\
\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{c}}&=\Bigl(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}||\mathcal{B}|}\sum_{p,b}\bigl(\min\{\widehat{\PL},L_{\max}\}-\min\{\PL,L_{\max}\}\bigr)^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:plmetrics}
\end{equation}
the first over the ray-traced links (with the regression part for a hurdle
model) and the second, censored, over every pair; the link-existence and
association metrics are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{prec}&=\frac{|\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\cap\mathcal{L}|}{|\widehat{\mathcal{L}}|},\qquad
\mathrm{rec}=\frac{|\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\cap\mathcal{L}|}{|\mathcal{L}|},\\
A_{\mathrm{serv}}&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{r}}|}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{r}}}\ind\{\hat s(p)=s(p)\},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:assoc}
\end{equation}
so that a pixel the model does not reach counts as wrongly associated, and
the reach accuracy is the share of pixels whose reached state
$\ind\{\exists b:(p,b)\in\mathcal{L}\}$ is predicted correctly. Coverage
is compared at $L\in\{120,130,140\}$\,dB through the agreement
$|\mathcal{P}|^{-1}\sum_p\ind\{\hat c_L(p)=c_L(p)\}$ and the error
$\hat C_L-C_L$ of the covered share; the SINR of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sinr}, with
values below \SI{\sinrFloor}{\decibel} (including pixels at which no site is
predicted to be received) set to \SI{\sinrFloor}{\decibel} for the truth and
every predictor alike, is compared through the mean absolute error and the
Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance of the two empirical distributions,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{SINR}}&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_p\bigl|\widehat{\mathrm{SINR}}(p)-\mathrm{SINR}(p)\bigr|,\\
D_{\mathrm{KS}}&=\sup_x\bigl|\hat F_{\widehat{\mathrm{SINR}}}(x)-\hat F_{\mathrm{SINR}}(x)\bigr|,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sinrmetrics}
\end{equation}
and the spectral efficiency of Eq.~\eqref{eq:se} through the error of its
mean. The cell edges are compared by the symmetric mean distance between
the predicted and the true edge sets and by the relative error of the
number of edge pixels, a proxy for the handover rate along a route,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}&=\frac12\Bigl[\frac{1}{|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}|}\sum_{e\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}\min_{e'\in\mathcal{E}}\lVert e-e'\rVert
+\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\min_{e'\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}\lVert e-e'\rVert\Bigr],\\
\rho_{\mathcal{E}}&=\frac{|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}|-|\mathcal{E}|}{|\mathcal{E}|}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:edges}
\end{equation}

Selecting $K$ of the $|\mathcal{B}|$ sites to maximise the number of covered
pixels is a monotone submodular maximisation, for which the greedy algorithm
attains a $(1-1/e)$ approximation of the optimum of the function it is
given~\cite{nemhauser1978}; site selection with propagation models is a
classical planning task~\cite{amaldi2003}. With $C_{140}(S)$ the share of
pixels covered at \SI{140}{\decibel} by a set $S$ of sites, the greedy plan
on a map is $S_{0}=\varnothing$ and
\begin{equation}
S_{k}=S_{k-1}\cup\Bigl\{\argmax_{b\in\mathcal{B}\setminus S_{k-1}}C_{140}(S_{k-1}\cup\{b\})\Bigr\},
\qquad k=1,\dots,K,
\label{eq:greedy}
\end{equation}
run once on the \emph{predicted} map, giving $\hat S_K$, and once on the
\emph{true} map, giving $S_K^{\star}$; both are scored on the true map, and
the planning regret is
\begin{equation}
R_K=C_{140}^{\mathrm{true}}(S_K^{\star})-C_{140}^{\mathrm{true}}(\hat S_K).
\label{eq:regret}
\end{equation}
For dimensioning, the number of sites the greedy order on the predicted map
needs to cover \covTarget\% of the pixels is also counted, together with the
true coverage those sites achieve.

A planner must also decide which pixels to \emph{promise} as covered. For a
transfer model and a held-out city $c$, let
\begin{equation}
r_p=\min\{\PL(p,s(p)),L_{\max}\}-\widehat{\PL}(p,\hat s(p))
\label{eq:resid-cert}
\end{equation}
be the true serving path loss of pixel $p$, censored at the cut-off of the
corpus, minus the predicted one, and $q_c$ the $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of
$\{r_p\}$ over the pixels of $c$. The censoring keeps $q_c$ finite in a city
where more than a fraction $\alpha$ of the pixels is reached by no site;
where the model itself predicts no reaching site, $r_p=-\infty$ and the
pixel is never claimed.

\begin{proposition}[City-level coverage certificate]
\label{prop:certify}
Let the $n$ cities be exchangeable and the training procedure symmetric in
its source cities. With $q_c$ computed from the leave-one-city-out model of
each city $c$, let $\hat q_t$ be the $j$-th largest of $\{q_c\}_{c\ne t}$ for
a target city $t$, and claim pixel $p$ as covered at level $L<L_{\max}$ when
$\widehat{\PL}(p,\hat s(p))+\hat q_t\le L$. Then, with probability at least
$(n-j)/n$ over the draw of the target city, at most a fraction $\alpha$ of
its pixels are claimed but not covered.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each $q_c$ is a function of city $c$ and of the multiset of the other
cities, through the same symmetric procedure, so exchangeable cities give an
exchangeable vector $(q_1,\dots,q_n)$. The event that $q_t$ exceeds the
$j$-th largest of the other values means that $q_t$ is among the $j$ largest
of the vector, with no tie at the $j$-th place; by exchangeability its
probability is at most $j/n$, hence $\Pr(q_t\le\hat q_t)\ge(n-j)/n$. On this
event, a claimed pixel with $r_p\le\hat q_t$ has a finite prediction and
satisfies $\min\{\PL(p,s(p)),L_{\max}\}=\widehat{\PL}(p,\hat s(p))+r_p\le\widehat{\PL}(p,\hat s(p))+\hat q_t\le L<L_{\max}$,
hence $\PL(p,s(p))\le L$ and the pixel is covered. Claimed but uncovered
pixels therefore have $r_p>\hat q_t\ge q_t$, and by the definition of the
quantile at most a fraction $\alpha$ of the pixels satisfy this.
\end{proof}

\noindent The guarantee is at the level of cities, the unit of shift, in the
spirit of conformal prediction~\cite{vovk2005,barber2021}; it needs no model
of the residuals, and the nested selection of Section~\ref{sec:operators}
provides the required symmetry. The rank $j$ trades confidence for
coverage: $j=1$ (the largest margin of the other cities) gives probability
$(n-1)/n$, $j=2$ gives $(n-2)/n$ with a smaller margin. The certificate is
reported at $L=\SI{140}{\decibel}$ for $\alpha\in\{0.05,0.10\}$ and
$j\in\{1,2\}$ (the results at \SI{130}{\decibel} are released with the code)
with the claimed and the falsely claimed share of the pixels, and compared
with the naive claim $\widehat{\PL}(p,\hat s(p))\le L$.

\subsection{Robustness analyses and inference}
\label{sec:robust}

Six analyses test whether the conclusions depend on the choices of the main
corpus, and one protocol governs every comparison. \emph{Mixed-path
composition.} The framework of~\cite{ozyurt2026}, following
MAPLE~\cite{ozyurt2020maple}, composes the loss of a link from every tile
its straight path crosses. The horizontal segment from each site to each
pixel is split at the tile borders (sampled every \SI{10}{\metre}), so that
tile $t$ holds a length $L_t$ of it, between the distances
$d_t^{\mathrm{in}}$ and $d_t^{\mathrm{out}}$ from the site, and two readings
of the composition are evaluated as a propagation law. The \emph{piecewise}
reading is a multi-slope law along the path and the \emph{path-averaged}
reading averages the parameters along the path,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_{L,\mathrm{pw}}&=A_{s}+\sum_t 10\gamma_t\log_{10}\frac{\max(d_t^{\mathrm{out}},d_0)}{\max(d_t^{\mathrm{in}},d_0)},\\
P_{L,\mathrm{avg}}&=\sum_t\frac{L_t}{d_h}\bigl[A_t+\gamma_t x\bigr],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mixed}
\end{equation}
with the intercept $A_s$ of the site's tile, the exponent of the tile each
stretch of distance lies in, and the horizontal length $d_h$. Both reduce
to Eq.~\eqref{eq:logdist} when the whole path lies in one tile. A third,
literal reading that sums one log-distance loss per crossed tile is
reported in Section~\ref{sec:res-mixed}. All are linear in the tile
parameters, which are fitted jointly to all links of a city by least squares
with a weak ridge penalty ($\lambda=10$) towards the best city-wide pair;
they are evaluated with tiles of \SI{100}{\metre} and, as
in~\cite{ozyurt2026}, of \SI{200}{\metre}, \SI{500}{\metre} and
\SI{1000}{\metre}, always on the pixels of the main corpus. The labels of
the tiles crossed by at least 30 links are transferred with the source
median, kernel transfer and GBDT under the nested protocol (one seed), and
the predicted maps are scored as above. \emph{Network size.} Every
zero-shot predictor is also scored on the network of the first ten sites
selected by the siting rule (one site per \SI{0.9}{\kilo\metre\squared},
inter-site distance about \SI{\isdSmall}{\metre}). \emph{Siting and
materials.} The models trained on the main corpus are applied unchanged to
the three replicates; the tile model is scored with labels fitted on the
replicate itself and with the labels of the same tiles from the main corpus.
\emph{Tile size.} Labels, descriptors, zero-shot transfer (median, ridge,
GBDT) and the network evaluation are repeated for $G_s\in\{50,100,200\}$\,m.
\emph{Censoring.} Links beyond the cut-off are missing, which can bias a
least-squares slope. The labels are refitted with the cut lowered to $140$
and \SI{130}{\decibel} and, for \num{300} random tiles per city, the
least-squares slope is compared with that of a censored-Gaussian (Tobit)
model~\cite{tobin1958} that also uses the (pixel, site) pairs beyond the
cut. \emph{Second band.} The tile and link pipelines are repeated at
\SI{\freqTwoGHz}{\giga\hertz}, and the \SI{\freqGHz}{\giga\hertz} link
models are applied to the second band with the free-space offset
$20\log_{10}(f_2/f_1)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fspl}, their knife-edge feature
evaluated at the new wavelength. \emph{External scenes.} The Munich,
\'Etoile and Florence scenes distributed with Sionna~RT were modelled
independently of the corpus of this study, with detailed meshes and several
ITU materials (brick, concrete, marble, metal, wood). Building footprints
and heights are derived from their meshes by vertical ray casting on a
\SI{2}{\metre} grid, \nExtSitesWord{} rooftop sites per scene are placed with
the siting rule of the main corpus (minimum separation
\SI{\extMinSep}{\metre}, the scenes being smaller), the scenes are ray
traced with the corpus settings, and the following predictors are evaluated
without any retraining: the tile labels, the source median, ridge and GBDT
fitted on all eleven cities under the nested rule, and the mean of the
eleven leave-one-city-out link models, zero-shot and with the per-site
calibration of Section~\ref{sec:fewshot} from $k\in\{10,25\}$ surveyed tiles
(ten random draws, and the two designs), scored on the unsurveyed pixels.
The San Francisco scene has terrain and would require a terrain-following
measurement surface; it is not used.

Every city is the target once (leave-one-city-out) and the other cities are
training cities, inside which the nested selection of
Section~\ref{sec:operators} takes place. Tiles of one city are spatially
correlated, so tile-level tests would overstate significance; the unit of
inference is the target city. For a comparison of two predictors with
per-city differences $\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_n$ of a metric, the mean
difference $\bar\Delta$ is reported with a 95\% percentile bootstrap
interval over cities~\cite{efron1993} from $B=\num{10000}$ resamples,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CI}_{95}=\bigl[\bar\Delta^{*}_{(0.025B)},\;\bar\Delta^{*}_{(0.975B)}\bigr],
\label{eq:boot}
\end{equation}
the exact two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank $p$-value~\cite{wilcoxon1945} and
the number of cities in which the first predictor is better. Within each
family of $m$ comparisons the $p$-values are ordered,
$p_{(1)}\le\dots\le p_{(m)}$, and Holm's step-down
correction~\cite{holm1979} rejects the hypotheses in that order while
\begin{equation}
p_{(i)}\le\frac{0.05}{m-i+1},
\label{eq:holm}
\end{equation}
stopping at the first failure. Standard deviations across cities use
$n-1$. With \nCities{} cities an exact Wilcoxon test can reach $p<0.05$ only
when nearly every city agrees; the power limit is stated where it matters.
