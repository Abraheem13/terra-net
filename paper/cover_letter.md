Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript **"Cross-city path-loss transfer for digital-twin
network planning: why tile parameters fall short and how site-aware link
models close the gap"** for consideration as a research article in
*Computer Communications*.

Tile-based path-loss models, which fit a log-distance exponent and intercept
per map tile and transfer them to unmeasured tiles by descriptor similarity,
were recently proposed for 6G digital twins in this journal (Ozyurt,
*Computer Communications* 257 (2026) 108620). That study used confidential
measurements from two cities. Our manuscript asks whether such transfer works
across many cities and what its errors cost a network planner, and answers it
on a public, fully reproducible benchmark.

Main contributions:

1. **Open multi-city corpora.** Eleven geo-referenced cities ray traced from
   220 rooftop sites at 3.5 GHz (8,977,400 links) and at 7.5 GHz, a
   random-siting replicate and independently modelled external scenes. Code,
   data and every number, table and figure are regenerated by one command.
2. **Evaluation where a planner uses the model.** Path loss, serving cell,
   SINR, coverage, cell edges, dimensioning and site planning, with city-level
   statistics (bootstrap intervals, exact Wilcoxon tests, Holm correction).
3. **Why tile parameters fall short.** The tile labels are imprecise (their
   cluster-robust uncertainty is 3.2 times the naive one) and depend on the
   serving sites and the dynamic range of the data. Even exact tile labels
   give a path-loss error of 11.6 dB and the correct serving site for only
   50.1% of the pixels. We evaluate the published framework as written and in
   two further readings of its mixed-path composition (piecewise and
   path-averaged), at tile sizes from 100 m to 1000 m; none closes the gap.
4. **Site-aware hurdle link transfer.** Predicting each link, and whether it
   exists, from its obstruction and diffraction geometry reduces the error on
   unseen cities to 9.3 dB, raises serving-site accuracy to 68.8% and cuts the
   coverage lost when planning five sites from 20.6% to 0.5%, in every
   held-out city. A survey of 25 random tiles calibrates it on scenes with
   other materials, and a distribution-free city-level certificate lets it
   promise 92.0% of the pixels as covered at a guaranteed risk (78.3% for tile
   transfer).

The work builds directly on, and tests, a framework published in *Computer
Communications*, and addresses the journal's readership in network planning,
digital twins and propagation modelling. All results come from ray tracing;
the manuscript states this limitation explicitly and explains why ray tracing
is a demanding testbed for tile transfer.

The manuscript is original, has not been published and is not under
consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission and declare
no competing interests. The use of AI-assisted tools is declared in the
manuscript. The code and data are available at
https://github.com/Abraheem13/terra-net.
