Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript **"Cross-city propagation-model transfer for
network planning in 6G digital twin networks: tile parameters versus
site-aware link models"** for consideration as a research article in
*Computer Communications*.

**Fit with the aims and scope.** The manuscript addresses the modelling and
simulation of next-generation mobile networks: it asks which propagation
model a 6G digital twin network should carry for cities where no
measurements exist, and answers the question at the level of the network
decisions the twin supports, namely cell association, SINR, coverage, cell
edges and handover, site dimensioning and site selection, together with a
statistical guarantee on the coverage an operator can promise. It thereby
falls within the journal's topics on the modelling, measurement and
simulation of computer communication networks, mobile and ubiquitous
networks, algorithmic aspects of communication networks and emerging
technologies for next-generation networks. The propagation model is treated
as an input of the digital twin network (ITU-T Y.3090) and is never studied
for its own sake: every result is scored through the planning quantities.
The work tests, on a public benchmark, a tile-transfer framework that this
journal published in 2026 (Ozyurt, *Computer Communications* 257, 108620),
which was evaluated on confidential measurements from two cities.

**Main contributions.**

1. **Open multi-city corpora and a reproducible benchmark.** Eleven
   geo-referenced cities ray traced from 220 rooftop sites at 3.5 GHz
   (8,977,400 links) and in the upper mid-band at 7.5 GHz, three replicates
   (random siting, mid-rise siting, random building materials), a
   solver-convergence study and independently modelled external scenes.
   Code, data and every number, table and figure are regenerated by one
   command; every hyper-parameter is selected inside the training cities.
2. **Evaluation where a planner uses the model.** Path loss, serving site,
   SINR, coverage, cell edges, dimensioning and site planning over the
   complete receiver grid including locations no site reaches, with
   city-level statistics (bootstrap intervals, exact Wilcoxon tests, Holm
   correction).
3. **Why tile parameters fall short.** The tile labels are imprecise (their
   cluster-robust uncertainty is 3.2 times the naive one) and depend on the
   serving sites and the dynamic range of the data. Even exact tile labels
   give a path-loss error of 11.6 dB and the correct serving site for only
   50.1% of the locations. The gap is decomposed into the missing
   reachability model, the line-of-sight state and the geometry of each
   link, and the published framework is evaluated as written and in two
   further readings of its mixed-path composition at tile sizes from 100 m
   to 1000 m; none closes the gap.
4. **Site-aware hurdle link transfer.** Predicting each link, and whether it
   exists, from its obstruction and diffraction geometry reduces the error on
   unseen cities to 9.3 dB, raises the serving-site accuracy to 68.4% and
   cuts the coverage lost when planning five sites from 18.4% to 0.7%, in
   every held-out city; it also outperforms per-link physics baselines
   (knife-edge, Deygout, 3GPP UMa with geometric line of sight) in path loss
   and serving site. A survey of 25 random tiles calibrates it on scenes with
   other materials, and a distribution-free city-level certificate lets it
   promise 80.6% of the locations as covered at a guaranteed risk (48.4% for
   tile transfer).

All results come from ray tracing; the manuscript states this limitation
explicitly and explains why ray tracing is a demanding testbed for tile
transfer.
