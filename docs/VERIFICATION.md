# Verification report for the manuscript

*Cross-city propagation-model transfer for network planning in 6G digital twin
networks: tile parameters versus site-aware link models*

This report records how every element of the manuscript was checked, so that
each statement in the article can be traced to a script, a result file or a
bibliographic record. It accompanies the revision prepared for *Computer
Communications* and is regenerated by hand after each build; the automated
checks it cites are part of the repository and can be rerun with the commands
given below.

## 1. Journal fit

The aims and scope of *Computer Communications* (Elsevier, ISSN 0140-3664),
as published on the journal's ScienceDirect and guide-for-authors pages, state
that the journal covers all aspects of future computer communication networks
on all layers except the physical layer, with attention to the evolution of
Internet architecture, protocols, services and applications, and list among
its topics: Internet of Things; edge/cloud computing; green networking; mobile
and ubiquitous networks; terrestrial and non-terrestrial networks; mobile
network services and applications; experimental test-beds and research
platforms; algorithmic aspects of communication networks; emerging
technologies for next-generation networks; future Internet architecture,
protocols and services; modelling, measurement and simulation of computer
communication networks.

The manuscript was repositioned accordingly:

| Journal topic | Where the manuscript addresses it |
|---|---|
| Modelling, measurement and simulation of networks | Ray-traced corpora of eleven cities; network-level simulation of serving site, SINR, coverage, cell edges, dimensioning and site planning (Sections 3, 4.6, 5.4) |
| Mobile and ubiquitous networks; emerging technologies for next-generation networks | 6G digital twin networks (ITU-T Y.3090, IMT-2030) as the setting; architecture of the propagation-model layer (Fig. 1); upper mid-band corpus (Sections 1, 4.1, 5.8) |
| Algorithmic aspects of communication networks | Submodular site planning and survey design (Propositions 3 and 4, Sections 4.4 and 4.6), nested selection protocol (Fig. 3), city-level certificate |
| Experimental test-beds and research platforms | Open, reproducible benchmark regenerated by one command; cost stated (Section 5.1, Table 4) |

The propagation model is treated throughout as an input of the digital twin
network and is never studied for its own sake: every result is scored through
the planning quantities. The title, abstract, keywords, introduction and
conclusions state this framing.

## 2. Style alignment with the journal

Two recent articles of the journal were read in full as style references:
*Computer Communications* 260 (2026) 108679 and 108680. The manuscript was
aligned with them on the following points.

- Third-person voice throughout ("this article", "the proposed model"); no
  first-person pronoun remains in any section (checked by regular expression
  over `paper/sections/*.tex`).
- Structure: Introduction with motivation, open questions, a bulleted list of
  contributions and an organisation paragraph; Related work with three
  subsections and a feature-based comparison table (Table 1) ending with a
  positioning paragraph; System model with a notation table (Table 2);
  Methodology with seven subsections, 41 numbered equations, one algorithm
  and four propositions proved in place; Performance evaluation opening with
  an *Experimental setting* subsection that states hardware, software
  versions, a parameter table (Table 3), the cost of one reproduction
  (Table 4) and an enumerated list of evaluation metrics; Discussion;
  Conclusions and future work; CRediT, competing interests, funding, AI
  declaration, data availability, references. There is no appendix. Every
  section and subsection opens with a short paragraph that states what it
  contains and why; no sub-subsections or run-in paragraph headings are used.
- Tables: caption above, three horizontal rules, best value in bold where a
  comparison is made, units in the header.
- Figures: an architecture diagram of the propagation-model layer (Fig. 1),
  a pipeline diagram (Fig. 2) and a flowchart of the nested protocol
  (Fig. 3), all drawn in TikZ, and fifteen plain engineering plots of the
  results (Figs. 4--18); no decorative or illustrative imagery. Every multi-series figure carries a
  legend placed outside the data area, markers or line styles in addition to
  colour, and a colour palette validated for colour-vision deficiency (three
  hues, worst pairwise CVD contrast ΔE 9.2, normal-vision ΔE 24.0, plus
  achromatic tones). Every figure page was inspected after the final build;
  no legend, label or panel letter overlaps data or another element.

## 6. Statements checked against the regenerated numbers

Every number in the text is a macro from `paper/generated/numbers.tex`. The
direction-dependent statements were read against the regenerated values after
the final run; three were narrowed to what the data show and are now worded
accordingly:

- the link model outperforms the physics baselines in path loss and serving
  site, but not in the five-site plan, where the Deygout construction with the
  deterministic hurdle is as good (0.1% against 0.7% regret);
- the empirical-Bayes prior strength of the site calibration has a median of
  37 links, close to the fixed value of 50, so the calibration leaves the
  zero-shot model nearly unchanged on this corpus;
- few-shot correction of the tile exponent gains 0.6% with ten surveyed tiles
  and 1.9% with one hundred.

## 8. What this verification does not cover

- All results are ray traced; no measured data with known site positions were
  available. The manuscript states this limitation.
- The two bibliographic entries flagged above must be confirmed by the
  authors from the publisher's pages.
- Eleven cities limit the power of city-level tests and the resolution of the
  certificate; both limits are stated in the manuscript.
